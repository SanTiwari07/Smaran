## 3. Format evolution (important for final-round prep)

**Code Cubicle 5.0 format** (SOURCE-REPORTED, Unstop [U5], verbatim excerpts):

> "Online Round … 250 shortlisted teams will pitch their product/prototype live via Google Meet (5-min pitch + 2-min Q&A). Teams have to work on given problem statements only!"
> "Offline Finale … Top 15 teams will travel to Microsoft Office for an **8-hour on-site hackathon**, solve real-world problems, and present live to the jury. Travel Reimbursement won't be provided!"
> Rules: "All code must be written during the hackathon … No plagiarism or use of previously built projects."

**Code Cubicle 6.0** keeps the structure (online elimination → ~15–20 teams → offline final, 09:00–18:00) [S1].

**ANALYST INFERENCE:**

- The online round is probably a **5-minute pitch + 2-minute Q&A** again. Rehearse to exactly 5:00.
- The offline final may include **on-site build time**. Be ready to extend the product live, for example with a judge-requested feature or dataset.
- Travel is probably **not reimbursed**. Plan logistics for Noida on 11 Oct.

### Code Cubicle 4.0 and 5.0

**NOT FOUND — REQUIRES VERIFICATION.** No public winner list was found on Unstop, Devfolio or the organizer site. LinkedIn posts exist, but Bing began serving a CAPTCHA, which we did not bypass. One LinkedIn profile snippet mentions reaching "the Grand Finale of Code Cubicle 4.0 … out of 2000+ teams". **Action:** ask a Geek Room contact or search LinkedIn manually for "#codecubicle5".

### Patterns (ANALYST INFERENCE from CC3)

1. Winners paired **AI with a concrete human problem** (therapy, mental health, finance, misinformation). None was a pure infrastructure tool.
2. **Trust and verification** themes win ("what's true in a world of fakes"). Smaran's "memory that doesn't lie" is aligned.
3. A **hardware/IoT** entry won. Edge and embedded work is appreciated.
4. Judges were **corporate AI and data-science practitioners** (Mastercard AI Garage directors, a Kaggle Grandmaster). Expect questions about measured accuracy and methodology, not only UX.

## 6. What kind of project is this organizer likely to appreciate?

Each point is **ANALYST INFERENCE**, tied to the evidence above.

| Signal | Evidence | Implication for us |
|---|---|---|
| Profile-driven selection | "selected based on Github and Linkedin profiles" [S1] | The repo must look professional within 30 seconds: README hero, results table, CI badge, clear commit history, credits. Every member's LinkedIn should mention the project. |
| Corporate venues and hiring angle | Microsoft, Mastercard, Paytm offices; "hiring opportunities" [S3] | Present as engineers solving a real operational problem, with measured results |
| Real-world AI framing | Theme "AI-Powered Intelligence for the Real World"; CC3 winners were human-problem AI | Lead with the technician and the machine, not with "version vectors" |
| Growing sponsor sophistication | Web3 sponsors (2024) → Groq (2025) → Pathway (title, 2025) → Qdrant / Pathway / Cloudinary (2026) | Deep correct use of the sponsor technology (Qdrant Edge) will be noticed |
| Fast, energetic event culture | Reviews praise mentoring and management [G1] | Be polished and time-disciplined; respect the 5-minute slot |
